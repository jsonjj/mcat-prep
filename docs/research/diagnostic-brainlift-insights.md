# Diagnostic BrainLift — Insights (DOK 3)

Seven patterns that emerge from holding several knowledge-tree entries together. Each cites the
entries it synthesises; none restates a single entry. Citations resolve in
`diagnostic-brainlift-tree.md`.

---

**1. Length pays off mainly for the claim the contract forbids.**
Doubling a cognitive-diagnostic CAT from five items to ten buys **22 points of pattern-level
accuracy and about 6 of attribute-level accuracy** (tree 2.6), and the same asymmetry recurs on this
instrument: going from 200 items to 140 costs **1.6 points** of worst-world reproducibility, while
narrowing the report from three priorities to two bought **13.2** (tree 2.19). Items are the
expensive way to buy accuracy on whole-profile claims, and whole-profile claims are the ones no
feasible length can support anyway — an identical 10-way ranking reproduces **0% of the time even at
80 items per concept** (tree 2.18). The cheap lever is the size of the claim, and it is the only
lever with no item cost.

**2. No dimension in the delivered form clears the design's own display threshold.**
The output contract refuses to display a number for any dimension with fewer than **~20 items**
behind it (tree 2.26); the sitting supplies **14 items per concept** (tree 2.25); and contention
allocation — the feature that recovers most of the gap against item-level adaptive — lowers the hard
per-concept guarantee from **14 to 8** (tree 1.20). The ban on per-concept scores is therefore not a
choice among reportable options: at 140 items over ten concepts there is no compliant way to show
one. The mechanism that buys reproducibility moves the form further from the threshold, so on this
axis precision and displayability run in opposite directions.

**3. The bank's worst deficit as an exam simulator is what qualifies it as a placement instrument.**
**2,108 items across 2,108 distinct family IDs**, median passage length **158 characters**, means
there are no passage sets at all, so a credible full-length cannot be assembled from this material
(tree 2.25). That same absence removes the constraint that makes long passage-based tests
undiagnostic: when the unit of assembly is a passage generating 4–7 items, the passage decides what
those items are about, forfeiting per-concept guarantees and within-concept difficulty balance at
any length (tree 2.16). One property of the bank, read as a content gap in one frame and as free
item-level assembly in the other.

**4. The adaptivity dial worth paying for is the one the thinnest pool can least afford to turn.**
About **80% of multistage's deficit** against item-level selection is allocation — which concepts
the items are spent on — while doubling the routing decisions from three to six is worth **0.3
points** (tree 1.20). Allocation works by concentrating items on contended concepts, and the bank is
lopsided enough that the contended concept may be FC10, which holds **40 items against FC4's 414**
and already yields up to **24** of them to a single 140-item sitting (tree 2.25). MST's gentler
exposure — **131–224 items used against item-level CAT's 530 of 600** (tree 1.2) — is then not a
side benefit of the delivery model but the precondition for using the valuable dial at all.

**5. Three decisions in three different layers are one labelling defect.**
Skill and cognitive complexity are almost perfectly confounded in the bank — **SIRS1 is 100% DOK1,
SIRS3 is 88% DOK3**, and only **6 of 114 lessons are skill-pure** (tree 2.25). That single
measurement is why the output contract emits no operation read at all (tree 2.26), why the crossed
content × operation version of the prescription claim had to be retracted (tree 2.30, Claim 9), and
why "weak at SIRS3" and "weak at hard items" cannot be separated by any amount of modelling.
Relabelling the bank is a single project that would release all three, which is invisible from any
one of them.

**6. Whether the attrition evidence indicts this instrument turns on what counts as the first
assessment.**
Stopout at the first graded assessment averages **18.71% against 7.27%** thereafter, and item count
on that assessment predicts it upward (**+0.073**) while expected time commitment predicts it
*downward* (**−0.0495**) before flipping positive by the second assessment (tree 3.3). Staged
delivery changes no item and no budget — it changes which object occupies that position: a **20-item
block in about half an hour**, interleaved with lessons, rather than a 140-item sitting (tree 3.9).
The opposite signs also rule out the naive mitigation: what raises stopout is the salient item count
rather than the time asked for, so a short block presented as purposeful is better aimed than a
merely shorter test.

**7. The diagnostic matters most to the learner its safety nets protect least.**
A learner who covers 3 of 10 concepts never encounters their true worst **10.4%** of the time,
against half a percent at seven or more (tree 2.28). The same runway governs the other repair
mechanism: adding a placement test to accumulated history raises explained variance by **~6 points
in math and under 2 in English**, and history alone beat the test outright (tree 2.13) — but history
accrues only to learners who keep studying, and **one lesson already out-measures the diagnostic**
for its concept (tree 2.25). Placement's measured benefit is front-loaded in the same direction,
**+16pp and +11pp at three semesters against +7pp and +8pp at nine** (tree 2.12), so the
short-runway learner draws the largest benefit from a correct read and has the least protection
against a wrong one.
