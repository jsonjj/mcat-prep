# Prep Pattern Snapshot validation plan

The 12-question Snapshot is a lead diagnostic and conversation starter. It is not a scaled-score
predictor, readiness test, broad placement, or substitute for a full-length exam.

## What is verified now

- Server-only answer keys and one-use scoring attempts.
- Six paired observations remain visible in every report.
- A positive repeated data signal requires 4/4 keyed matches.
- A repeated friction feature requires at least three matching answer features spanning both data
  subpairs.
- Failed pairs are prioritized before mixed or positive evidence.
- Automated coverage includes all 256 data-answer combinations, 96 within-pair answer
  combinations, and 20,736 scoring equivalence profiles.
- The current form uses multi-step energy, force-balance, controlled-assay, multi-curve,
  initial-rates, perturbation, and time-course interpretation.

These checks verify deterministic behavior, not psychometric quality.

## What requires target-learner data

Run a consented pilot with learners across preparation stages and recent practice-score bands.
Analyze:

- item percent-correct and perfect-form rate;
- response time, omissions, and device effects;
- option-selection frequencies and nonfunctioning distractors;
- item discrimination against a separate criterion such as recent relevant full-length section
  performance;
- within-pair association and test–retest stability;
- whether coded answer features match learner reasoning in think-aloud interviews;
- report comprehension, perceived usefulness, and whether the suggested retest changes the next
  study action; and
- accessibility and differential performance across relevant learner groups.

Do not make items harder merely to suppress perfect scores. Revise or replace items when the form
shows ceiling effects, weak discrimination, ambiguous wording, inaccessible representations, or
distractors that do not attract the intended partial reasoning.

## Release boundary

Before public diagnostic claims:

1. independent scientific and originality review;
2. blinded solves and accessibility review;
3. versioned pilot form and scoring rules;
4. a predefined analysis plan and held-out confirmation sample;
5. published scope, uncertainty, and known omissions; and
6. no score, readiness, or improvement claim beyond the collected evidence.
